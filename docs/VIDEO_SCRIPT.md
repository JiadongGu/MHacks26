# Pulse demo video script (3:30–4:00)

| Time | On screen | Voiceover |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 0:00–0:20 | Dark screen. Three phone icons appear: Fitbit, a health record app, Google Calendar — each in its own corner, never touching. Cut to a Fitbit app: steps, heart rate, sleep, all sitting unread in a stats screen. | Your watch tracks your heart, your steps, your sleep. But that data just sits in an app. Your health record is in a different app. Your calendar is in a third. None of them talk to each other. Your wearable can see that you are getting sick, and nobody acts on it. |
| 0:20–0:50 | Web app. Sign up, then "Connect my health record" (FinchNode Connect). Digital twin card fills in: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, metformin, lisinopril, HbA1c 6.4%. Blood pressure threshold tightens from default to 130/80. Connect Google Calendar. Pick up to 3 focus areas. Phone: text PULSE-XXXXXX to the Pulse iMessage line. Reply: "Welcome to Pulse." | Setup takes about two minutes. Sign up, then connect your health record through FinchNode. Pulse reads your chart and builds your digital twin: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, metformin, lisinopril, HbA1c at 6.4. Because of the hypertension, Pulse tightens your blood pressure threshold to 130 over 80. Next: connect Google Calendar, pick up to three focus areas, and text the PULSE code to Pulse's iMessage line. Welcome to Pulse. |
| 0:50–1:20 | Dashboard, /demo panel. Press "Workout now". Live heart-rate chart climbs toward 154 bpm. A few seconds later, iMessage notification from Pulse: "Great job on that 15 minute workout, peak 154 bpm." | Now the live loop. In the demo panel I press Workout now. Heart rate streams into Pulse's live pool, and within seconds I get a text: "Great job on that 15 minute workout, peak 154 bpm." That message was written by Gemini, from your live data. |
| 1:20–2:10 | iMessage from Pulse: "Your resting heart rate is 84, your baseline is 74. You slept 5 hours. Your EECS 281 Midterm Exam is tomorrow at 2:00 PM. I can add a sleep block from 11:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Reply YES." Tap YES. Reply: "Done. Sleep block (Pulse) is on your calendar." Cut to Google Calendar: event visible under the "Pulse Health" calendar. | This is where Pulse earns its keep. It notices illness onset: resting heart rate 84 against a baseline of 74, only five hours of sleep, and your EECS 281 Midterm Exam is tomorrow at 2:00 PM. It texts you and proposes a sleep block, 11:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Nothing touches your real schedule until you reply YES. The moment I do, the event appears in a dedicated Pulse Health calendar in Google Calendar. Routine daily plans go into that same calendar, which you can hide if you do not want to see them. But anything that changes your real schedule needs your yes. |
| 2:10–2:40 | iMessage conversation. User: "how did I sleep?" Pulse answers with last night's hours and heart rate. User: "block sleep tonight" — Pulse proposes tonight's block and asks for YES. Cut to browser: the same agent in ASI:One (Fetch.ai Agentverse) answering "how did I sleep?" for the linked user. | Pulse also just talks. I text "how did I sleep?" and it pulls last night's numbers and tells me. "Block sleep tonight," and it proposes a block and waits for my yes. The same agent is published in ASI:One, Fetch.ai's Agentverse — ask it the same question there and you get the same answer. |
| 2:40–3:00 | Dashboard. Morning briefing card. Press Play — an ElevenLabs voice reads the briefing. Below it: Today's plan card with the day's blocks. | In the morning, the dashboard shows a briefing card. Press Play and Pulse reads it out loud, in a voice. Below it sits today's plan, built from your sleep, your vitals, and your calendar. |
| 3:00–3:20 | iMessage. User: "I have crushing chest pain". Immediate reply: "Call 911 now." User asks for another user's data by ID. Pulse: "I can only see your own health and calendar data." | Safety is built in, not bolted on. If you text "I have crushing chest pain," the reply — call 911 now — is deterministic code. It never depends on the model. Ask it for someone else's data and it refuses. We tested Gemini against 141 live cases and fixed the failures we found. |
| 3:20–3:50 | Architecture diagram builds left to right: Fitbit (Google Health API) and a simulated Apple Watch, into the FastAPI agent; SpacetimeDB live pool and Neon Postgres long-term store (Neon Auth); rules engine and scheduled jobs; Gemini with structured output, function calling, and model fallback; out to the Photon iMessage gateway, the web dashboard on Vercel, and a Fetch.ai uAgent in ASI:One; plus Google Calendar, FinchNode, ElevenLabs. Closing card: "Wellness guidance, not medical advice." Team card: J (Jiadong Gu), P (Gavin Mordhorst). | How it is built. Fitbit, through the Google Health API, and a simulated Apple Watch feed a Python FastAPI agent. Live vitals land in SpacetimeDB; long-term data in Neon Postgres. A rules engine and scheduled jobs decide what matters, and Gemini handles the words, with a model fallback when the primary model hits its limit. Out the other side: iMessage through Photon, the dashboard on Vercel, and a Fetch.ai agent in ASI:One. This is wellness guidance, not medical advice. I'm Jiadong Gu, and this is Gavin Mordhorst. This is Pulse. |
